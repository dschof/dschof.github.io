Dan Schofield

Researcher and Schmidt AI in Science Fellow
Visual Geometry Group, University of Oxford

I’m a researcher at the University of Oxford in the Visual Geometry Group (VGG), working with Professor Andrew Zisserman. My research applies computer vision and machine learning to unlock large-scale video data for research on cognition and behaviour.

My background is in biological anthropology, and studying how we evolved has shaped how I think about AI. I’m increasingly interested in what biology can teach us about building adaptive, safe systems: machines that adapt and learn as animals do, and that ultimately help people and the planet flourish.

Outside work I’m usually hiking somewhere in nature or making coffee.

Interests

Computational ethology · AI for science · Adaptive and embodied systems

Building

  • Ethologic Alongside my research at Oxford, I’ve started a research lab and company building adaptive autonomous robots inspired by natural intelligence. In its first year we completed three successful field deployments, with robots running multimodal recognition on edge hardware in remote, unstructured environments.
  • AnimalMatch With a team of research software engineers, I’m building a platform that helps researchers deploy AI models for individual recognition in field research and biodiversity monitoring. Backed by Schmidt Sciences.
